The research assistant market

Where predoctoral and research assistant positions in economics are posted.

  • EconJobMarketeconjobmarket.orgThe central market for research assistantships, predoctoral positions and doctoral fellowships in economics.
  • PREDOC Opportunitiespredoc.orgPredoctoral research positions across economics and quantitative social science, with application timelines.
  • NBER research assistant positionsnber.orgResearch assistant openings with NBER affiliated economists outside the NBER itself.
  • AEA Job Openings for Economistsaeaweb.orgThe American Economic Association's listings, including research and predoctoral posts alongside faculty jobs.

Applying to graduate school

Honest, practical advice on PhD applications in economics.

  • EconGradAdvicesites.google.comPractical advice on applying to economics PhD programmes, from preparation to choosing an offer.
  • Econ PhD Advicegavinjqu.github.ioA community maintained, open source collection of resources for graduate students in economics and undergraduates considering a PhD.

Coding and empirical methods

How to organise a project, write code that others can run, and think about identification. The workflow links are selected from Jonathan Dingel's advice page.

  • Code and Data for the Social Sciencesstanford.eduGentzkow and Shapiro's guide to organising reproducible research projects. Short, and worth rereading every year.
  • R for Data Sciencer4ds.hadley.nzWickham, Çetinkaya-Rundel and Grolemund's standard introduction to data analysis with the tidyverse in R.
  • Data Science for Economistsgithub.comGrant McDermott's EC607 lecture notes covering R, version control and the modern empirical workflow.
  • IPA data cleaning guidepovertyaction.github.ioInnovations for Poverty Action's standards for cleaning survey data reproducibly.
  • The Effecttheeffectbook.netNick Huntington-Klein's free textbook on research design and causal inference, with code in R, Stata and Python.
  • Causal Inference: The Mixtapemixtape.scunning.comScott Cunningham's free textbook on the main quasi experimental designs, with worked code.
  • Mixtape Sessionsgithub.comSlides, code and exercises from the Mixtape Sessions workshops: causal inference with Scott Cunningham, advanced difference in differences with Jonathan Roth, instrumental variables and shift share designs with Peter Hull, synthetic control with Alberto Abadie, and more.
  • Project templategithub.comJonathan Dingel's template for a research project organised as tasks and run with Make.
  • Git versus Dropboxmichaelstepner.comMichael Stepner on why a research project belongs under version control.
  • Why your research project needs build automationtradediversion.netJonathan Dingel's case for Make and reproducible pipelines.
  • The targets R packageropensci.orgThe user manual for targets, build automation for analysis pipelines in R.
  • project for Stataideas.repec.orgA Stata module for build automation, the Stata counterpart to Make.
  • The Plain Person's Guide to Plain Text Social Scienceplain-text.coKieran Healy on writing papers, code and slides in plain text.
  • gslab-econgithub.comGentzkow and Shapiro's lab infrastructure, the working counterpart to Code and Data.
  • The Task Is A Quantum Of Workflowhrdag.orgPatrick Ball on splitting a project into small, self contained tasks.
  • Principled Data Processingyoutube.comPatrick Ball's talk on the same approach to data work.
  • Why I encourage econ PhD students to learn Juliatradediversion.netJonathan Dingel's argument for Julia over Matlab.
  • Stata gotchasifs.org.ukAn Institute for Fiscal Studies note on Stata pitfalls that are easy to miss.

Presenting

Figures, tables, slides and talks. Selected from Jonathan Dingel's advice page.

  • The Visual Display of Quantitative Informationedwardtufte.comEdward Tufte's classic on presenting data coherently and efficiently. A book.
  • An Economist's Guide to Visualizing Dataaeaweb.orgJonathan Schwabish in the Journal of Economic Perspectives, 2014. The minimum standard for figures.
  • The principle of proportional inkcallingbullshit.orgThe ink in a figure should be proportional to the numbers it shows.
  • Assertion evidence slide designassertion-evidence.comMichael Alley's approach: put the claim in the slide title and support it with visual evidence.
  • Presentation noteskimjruhl.comKim Ruhl on why slides should be designed to be robust.
  • Regression tablesblogs.ubc.caKeith Head's criteria for presenting regression tables.
  • A Graph is Worth a Thousand Citationsericzwick.comEric Zwick on the case for good graphs.
  • Beamer tipsgithub.comPaul Goldsmith-Pinkham's tips for cleaner Beamer slides.
  • How To Speakyoutube.comPatrick Winston's lecture on giving talks.
  • Public Speaking for Academic Economistsdropbox.comRachel Meager's guide to speaking in seminars.
  • How to Give a Lunch Talkbu.eduAdam Guren's suggestions for internal lunch talks.
  • What Makes for a Successful Paper and Seminar?columbia.eduDon Davis's list of questions every presentation should answer.
  • The discussant's artchrisblattman.comChris Blattman on being a useful discussant: do not repeat what the presenter said.

Writing

How to write a paper people can read. Selected from Jonathan Dingel's advice page.

  • Writing mattersdoi.orgFeld, Lines and Ross, Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization, 2024. Experimental evidence on the value of editing an economics paper.
  • Models, Measurement, and the Language of Empirical Economicsdropbox.comPhil Haile on using language carefully in empirical work.
  • The introduction formulablogs.ubc.caKeith Head's formula for writing the introduction of a paper.
  • Writing Tips for PhD Studentsjohnhcochrane.comJohn Cochrane's guide to writing a paper.
  • The Sense of Stylestevenpinker.comSteven Pinker on clear prose and the curse of knowledge. A book.
  • Why Academics Stink at Writingweb.archive.orgSteven Pinker's essay on the same theme.
  • The Little Book of Research Writingbooks.google.comVaranya Chaubey's short guide to structuring a research paper. A book.
  • Things I hateblogs.ubc.caKeith Head's list of habits to drop from papers.

The academic job market

For later, but worth knowing early. Selected from Jonathan Dingel's advice page.

  • Guide and Advice for Economists on the US Junior Academic Job Marketaeaweb.orgJohn Cawley's comprehensive guide. Read it at least a year before going on the market.
  • How to do a job market spielowenzidar.wordpress.comOwen Zidar on the opening pitch, with examples from Justin Wolfers.
  • Guide to the European Job Marketdavid-schindler.deDavid Schindler on how the European market differs from the American one.
