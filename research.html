Reform by Stealth: A Three-Month Assessment of India's Labour Codes

India's four labour codes, in force since 21 November 2025, consolidate 29 central laws and are presented as the modernisation of a colonial era regulatory framework. Abstract

India's four labour codes, implemented on 21 November 2025, consolidate 29 central laws and are presented as a modernisation of a colonial era regulatory framework. This article argues that the codes effect a fundamental recalibration of relations between state, capital and labour rather than a mere administrative simplification.

Using Annual Survey of Industries data, Periodic Labour Force Survey indicators, and a comparative analysis of central and state level rules, the article shows that raising the retrenchment approval threshold from 100 to 300 workers exempts the overwhelming majority of industrial units from government scrutiny. In the 2023 to 2024 round of the Annual Survey of Industries, 86.8 percent of factories employ fewer than 200 workers and 94.0 percent fewer than 500, which places the share below the new 300 worker threshold at roughly 90 percent.

The replacement of labour inspectors with Inspector cum Facilitators shifts the enforcement paradigm from deterrence to self certification. The formal recognition of gig workers under the Social Security Code remains symbolic without enforceable rights on wages, hours, or algorithmic transparency. With central rules still in draft as of February 2026 and most states yet to notify final rules, the codes risk becoming instruments for the systematic erosion of labour rights rather than their modernisation.

Environmental Regime Uncertainty: measuring firm exposure to extraterritorial environmental regulation

Principal investigator: Dr Raphael Cunha, King's College London, with Fundação Getulio Vargas, São Paulo.

A news text based index of Environmental Regime Uncertainty: how firms in Brazil and Indonesia are exposed to extraterritorial rules such as the EU Deforestation Regulation, following Baker, Bloom and Davis. I build the index and the firm by sector panels in R, and run the difference in differences and event study analysis that links uncertainty to exports, filings and market reactions.

Does Telework Widen the Abstract-Task Wage Premium? Evidence from Within-Occupation Variation

Does the option to work from home pay more for abstract work? Using CPS microdata from 2019 to 2023 and O*NET task data, the dissertation finds a wage premium of about 2.5 percent concentrated among workers whose jobs can be done remotely.

Research areas

The questions I keep returning to, and the ones I am picking up.

Labour markets and the organisation of work

How remote work, task content and firm size shape wages, and who gains from flexibility. My dissertation measured the telework premium within occupations.

The political economy of regulation

Who bears the cost of regulatory change, and how thresholds, enforcement design and uncertainty shape what firms do. Current work covers India's labour codes and firm exposure to extraterritorial environmental rules.

Climate inequality

A newer interest: how climate damage and climate policy fall unevenly across workers, firms and places, and what regulation does to that distribution.

Technology and economics

How artificial intelligence and digital technology reshape tasks, careers and the distribution of gains, starting with higher education and early careers.
