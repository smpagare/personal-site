Why India's labour codes are reform by stealth

A plain language summary of my article, revised and resubmitted to Economic and Political Weekly.

On 21 November 2025 India brought four labour codes into force, folding 29 central laws into one framework. The government calls this modernisation. Three months of evidence suggest something more consequential: a quiet rebalancing of power between the state, employers and workers.

Three changes carry most of the weight. The threshold at which a factory needs government approval to retrench workers rises from 100 to 300, which places roughly nine in ten industrial units beyond routine scrutiny. Labour inspectors become Inspector cum Facilitators, so enforcement moves from deterrence to self certification. And gig workers gain formal recognition without enforceable rights over wages, hours or the algorithms that allocate their work.

Whether this becomes modernisation or erosion depends on rules that had not yet been written when the article went to review: central rules were in draft and most states had not notified final rules. The article sets out what to watch as those rules land.
